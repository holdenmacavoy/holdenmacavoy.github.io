About

About me

I grew up in Norwell, Massachusetts, just south of Boston, and moved across the country for UC San Diego. I'm finishing a B.S. in Economics at Muir College with a minor in mathematics.

My coursework leans quantitative. Alongside economics, I've taken proof-based math, nonlinear optimization and data science, so I'm comfortable going from a model on paper to an estimate in R or Python. I like problems where the economics and the numbers have to agree.

In summer 2026 I studied abroad in Berlin and interned at Deepsense, where I did market research and technology due diligence for early-stage investment work. That summer made it concrete for me: I want a career that combines rigorous analysis with real capital decisions.

Outside of work I played varsity tennis, and I train regularly. I've also spent a lot of time teaching. Leading review sessions for UCSD economics courses has made me better at explaining ideas simply, which I think is underrated in research.

Why U.S. health care spending diverged from the OECD

In progressMacroeconomicsHealth economics

The U.S. spends far more on health care per person than other rich countries, and the gap has widened for decades. This project adapts the Solow and Romer growth models to ask whether that divergence looks like a difference in inputs, in technology adoption, or in how efficiently spending turns into health output.

In progressEconometricsFinance

GLP-1 drugs like semaglutide and tirzepatide are among the fastest-growing drug classes ever sold. How I research a company

  • Build scenarios, not a single target. Bear, base and bull cases with explicit revenue, margin and share-count assumptions.
  • Stress the capital structure. Dilution, debt maturities and GAAP losses matter most for high-growth companies.
  • Model correlation. Positions exposed to the same driver get analyzed together.
  • Look after tax. For income strategies like covered-call ETFs, I compare yield after California and federal tax.
